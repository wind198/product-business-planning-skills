# Idea brief — Robotics & Electronics Learning Platform

## Problem / opportunity
Confirmed founder direction: make electronics and robotics accessible to learners without relevant backgrounds through simulation-based teaching, homework and practical projects. The founder's observation about inadequate teaching in simulators is a hypothesis, not market validation.

Proposed mission: enable learners to independently design, implement, test and debug systems.

## Who experiences it
Candidate segments: software developers transitioning to embedded/robotics (proposed beachhead), nontechnical professionals, engineering students from other disciplines, adult hobbyists, and institutions (future B2B). No segment size or demand evidence yet.

## Why now and alternatives
Standalone simulators, online courses, physical kits and formal education are alternatives. SimReady offers potential physical assets; it does not replace MCU simulation. Native integration and license/embedding terms require verification.

## Solution hypotheses
Education-first, visual-first, project-based, mastery-based; browser-first and initially hardware-independent. Four labs: interactive concepts, electronics, firmware, robotics. Competency graph allows prior knowledge to be recognized. Lessons: motivation, explanation, exploration, guided practice, homework, assessment, debugging, application. Evaluate observable behavior, not exact source code.

## Highest-risk assumptions
See discovery hypotheses and experiments in market-analysis.md. Curriculum quality and production cost are central risks.

## Next decision
Initial audience, geography/language, business objective. All detailed scope and technical selections remain proposed.
