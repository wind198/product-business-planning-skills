# Business evidence and selective interviewing policy

## Classifications

- **confirmed**: user-authorized business reality or primary authenticated record. Must link source and scope.
- **observed**: what a dated source, analytics export, customer interview, or pinned code revision actually shows; don't infer future performance.
- **proposed**: agent-designed segment, value proposition, feature, price, strategy or plan; welcome in drafts, not approved facts.
- **hypothesis**: falsifiable belief about buyer behavior, demand or viability awaiting testing.
- **assumed**: explicitly accepted premise with owner, expiry and validation method.
- **disputed**: sources conflict; preserve competing claims and flag resolution.
- **unknown**: missing; track impact but avoid blank-form interviews.
- **superseded**: retained historical revision.

## Before a question

Resolve existing canonical knowledge (including previous sessions and related deliverables), search evidence, research public information and formulate a proposal. Only ask when an actual project fact/constraint or significant decision is required. Keep rounds to 1–3 targeted questions. A user correction supersedes old confirmed information with history; autonomous research cannot silently replace it.

## Specific safeguards

- **Market size**: document population, geography, segmentation, calculation, sources and date; never present invented TAM/SAM/SOM as research.
- **Pricing**: proposed prices are hypotheses; willingness to pay needs suitable interviews/transactions/experiments. Competitor price and list price are not customer WTP.
- **Customer pool**: separate hypothetical ICP/segment from verified leads. Do not create fictitious customer records. Minimize PII and respect access/legal obligations.
- **Finance**: label growth, margin, CAC, churn and revenue figures as scenarios, state units and formulas. A successful launch is not evidence of profitable scale.
- **MVP/PRD**: user stories are proposals until accepted; observed code behavior and deployment are different things.
- **Marketing**: positioning and channels are hypotheses until tested; conversion metrics require observation periods.
- **External claims**: source URL, retrieval/publication date, geography and evidence quality; distinguish provider claims and third-party analysis.
- **Decisions**: a recommendation is not an approval. Human authorization should be recorded for binding pricing, budgets, product scope and claims.

## Cross-artifact quality gate

Any significant statement should link to a fact/hypothesis/source/decision. Changed answers mark affected product artifacts for review. Resolve material conflicts before treating plans as approved. Structural tests certify syntax, never market feasibility.
